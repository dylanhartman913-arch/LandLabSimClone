# LL-39: transportation as access

Implemented October 25, 2026 in engine **0.19.0**. Enable **Settings → Experimental mechanics → Transportation as access**. New games and continuation leave it off. The checklist keeps **Transportation access** as its own constraint, while Market shows completed/blocked trips, mileage consumed, fares paid and emergency fares payable. [Measured comparisons](LL_39_COMPARISONS.md) reproduce 72 matched first-year runs.

## What changes

Under this gate, residents no longer request 150 miles a week, lose wellbeing for unmet transportation, or count transportation in their household coverage/self-reliance scores. Travel is requested by actual errands, allocated work and town journeys. Paid travel supplies access, without inventing vehicle production. Failed access blocks the relevant activity and appears in Needs and bottlenecks and the Almanac; it does not become a generic resident need.

| Activity | Trip demand | Alternative without enough owned mileage | What happens if access fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market orders, physical supply backstops, sale collection | One shared **10-mile errand per day**, only when an activity needs it | One shared **$25 delivery/collection** | Orders/sales stay queued; physical backstops cannot deliver. Piped utilities are not road deliveries. |
| On-site employment | Catalog transport-to-labor ratio × hours allocated to the job | **$0.50 per commute mile** | No work or wage from that job that day. No labor allocation means no commute charge. |
| Remote employment | No commute | None needed | Existing labor and operating constraints still apply. |
| Other recurring system supply access | Catalog travel rate scaled by its operating share | **$0.50 per mile** | The system cannot operate that day. |
| Necessary town departure or return | **10 person-miles per traveler**, one household journey | **$25 household ride** | Emergency fares may remain explicitly payable instead of stranding a zero-cash household. Blizzards delay return journeys; emergency departure remains available. |

Average Job and Part-Time Job both have 3.75 commute miles per allocated work hour (150/40 and 75/20). These are fractional shift equivalents under the engine's daily distribution of weekly work, not a new weekday calendar or one extra full commute for a tiny fraction of a shift. Remote Job has no catalog travel input and needs no commute.

A trip atomically uses its full mileage from available measured output, or pays its full alternative fare. Partial mileage is not mixed with a flat delivery ride. Trips sharing the market key do not charge or consume mileage again that day. Physical delivery backstops use the configured grocery/feed/fuel list; Farm Stand's access happens through sale collection rather than an idle weekly charge. Local barter with Ruth keeps its existing no-trip rule.

Errands run before commute allocation, with sales later in the day; that fixed ordering is deterministic. Fares must be available before earning that day's wage or sale proceeds. A delivery cannot consume the entire cash balance as a fare without leaving funds for goods; explicit orders remain queued. Under separate storage-pool rules, a completely full destination makes no trip and preserves queued orders. Road closures under LL-38 also block commuting and sale collection, alongside delivery suspension.

## Actual mileage and accounting

Vehicles keep their catalog inputs and production rules. As with other non-storable flow outputs, yesterday's actual production provides today's mileage; unused older mileage expires, so an idle or fuel-starved vehicle cannot accumulate a multi-day bank. New vehicles need one operating day before their mileage is available. End-of-day return journeys can use the newly measured output then available. Production, miles consumed, fares and attempted/completed access remain separate ledger quantities.

Trips have a saved daily record containing purpose, optional system, attempted miles, owned miles consumed, paid/deferred fare, completion and failure reason. Paid market alternatives appear in the existing delivery bills; commute/supply/town fares and payment of old emergency fares appear as **Travel fares**. Cash reconciles after end-of-day town returns as well as ordinary daily activity.

Only emergency town journeys can defer a fare. The optional saved `access.fareDue` liability is visible in Market and paid from available cash before the following day's errands. Partial repayment leaves the remainder due and never overdraws cash. It carries no interest and is independent of LL-37 credit; ordinary purchases and commuting cannot borrow this way. This keeps the earlier safe-town-recovery path reachable without granting a free ride. Return eligibility uses the existing whole-household survival trial.

## Checklist, forecasts and compatibility

Measured Transportation access reports **completed trip miles / attempted trip miles**, with paid alternatives included in completed access. With no trips, it says **No trips attempted**. This row is excluded from the household coverage and self-reliance averages, but a failed trip can still reduce real food supplies or wages. The other rows retain their actual-flow accounting. In a measured window using the access rule, legacy days without trip records do not import their flat resident transportation demand into this row.

Placed and catalog cards suppress flat recurring Transportation inputs and explain that trips are recorded separately. Annual planned/completed forecasts remove those flat inputs, show **No trip forecast**, and explicitly omit trip scheduling and fares. Use measured trips and bills to assess access costs; annual forecasts are not a travel budget. The raw workbook's human/job flows and spreadsheet parity remain unchanged.

No new action type is needed: settings, orders, placement, trades and the existing town rules produce deterministic trips. Saves fingerprint fares payable; daily trip records travel with the existing ledgers. JSON continuation and replay reproduce both paid trips and an actual zero-cash emergency departure with its unpaid fare. Turning the gate off freezes unpaid fares while restoring legacy transportation behavior; re-enabling resumes settlement. No automatic experimental migration is introduced. See [SAVES.md](SAVES.md).

All new fares and trip policies are **experimental game balance** in validated catalog overrides. No new dependencies, catalog systems or workbook edits were added.

## Controlled results

Across Front Range, Laramie and Asheville, Standard seed 1 and Real seeds 1–3, all paired remote, part-time and adapt routes have zero survival-short days. Removing the flat resident travel penalty improves the remote route's minimum wellbeing from 75.2 to 75.4 and frees a little more work: end cash rises $111–$1,920 across matched runs. It takes 58–72 actual errands, paying $1,450–$1,800 in fares without its own transport.

The on-site part-time route can now fund commutes rather than remaining constrained by the lack of owned transport: end cash rises $27,465–$27,655 while paying $3,403–$3,673 in trip fares. The adapt route records 729 completed commute/errand entries and no paid alternatives; its measured vehicle mileage covers access once the flat resident demand is gone. Its end cash rises about $4,618. There are no blocked trips or unpaid emergency fares on these ordinary routes; dedicated regression scenarios exercise those failures.

Score changes are partly a changed denominator and the removal of a wellbeing drift, not new physical production. These measurements do not change defaults or tune the catalog.

## Verification and limits

Regressions cover idle residents, both card previews and measured/forecast checklist rows, actual mileage versus one delivery for batched orders, failed fares preserving orders, allocated-hour commuting and paused/remote work, expiring mileage from a fuel-starved vehicle, shared orders/backstops/sales, blizzard work/sale blocking, score exclusion, zero-cash departure and safe return with fare liabilities, delayed return in a blizzard, partial liability payment, replay and JSON continuation. Catalog checks reject invalid fares/miles and unknown systems while preserving workbook human travel quantities. The browser flow enables the rule, checks the idle row, places two orders, records one paid errand and checks measured access in Market and the checklist.

Limits: no route map, trip-time labor, vehicle seats, fractional mileage plus taxi splits, weekday job calendar or marginal per-trip vehicle fuel calculation. Vehicle operating costs/output retain their catalog rates. One shared market errand does not distinguish destinations or cargo capacity. Town trips model necessary departures and returns, not discretionary social visits. Annual balance forecasts do not schedule trips or charge their fares. Experimental fare liabilities pause with the gate off.
