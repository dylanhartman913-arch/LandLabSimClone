# LL-33: separate storage pools and winter water

Implemented October 24, 2026 in engine **0.13.0**. This completes one ticket, LL-33. Enable **Settings → Experimental mechanics → Separate storage and winter water** to try it. New games and continuation migrations leave the setting off. [Measured comparisons](LL_33_COMPARISONS.md) reproduce 24 first-year runs plus focused winter and feed checks.

## Raw and drinking water

Water storage (rain barrels, tanks, cisterns, the pond, the Earthship) now holds **raw water only**, plus the catalog's 50 gal raw-water buffer. Drinking water lives in its own household containers: the catalog's 50 gal Drinking water buffer. Each pool overflows on its own, so fetching, rain or a market run can never spill safe water to make room, and a filter that outruns the household spills its own surplus instead of pushing raw water out. Auto-gather sizes fetching to raw room and boiling to the containers' room. The Work water-target warning reports both rooms. LL-40's potable water tank will add capacity to the drinking water pool; no catalog row provides it yet.

## Feed and hay storage

Hay and Chicken feed share a **Feed storage** pool measured in the catalog's Storage space (cu ft): free Storage space (the Toolshed Workshop's 800 cu ft less what tools occupy) plus 10 cu ft of household bins. Densities are about 9.5 lb/cu ft for small square hay bales and 40 lb/cu ft for bagged poultry feed. Excess spills like any pool. Market trips bring home only what fits at the time of the trip; the rest of a one-off order is dropped, and standing orders top up again on later trips. The Farm & Feed Store backstop feeds animals directly and is unaffected.

## Winter water

Above-ground tanks freeze: the Rain Barrel (tolerates 10 °F-days), IBC Tank (20), 550-gal cistern (30) and 5,000-gal cistern (80). The pond and the Earthship's built-in cisterns are treated as frost-protected. Each day's mean temperature is 65 °F less heating degree days plus cooling degree days. A tank's frost rises by the °F the day falls below 32 °F and melts by the °F it rises above, capped at 1.5× its tolerance, so a frozen tank thaws after a stretch of warmer days rather than staying locked until summer.

When frost reaches the tolerance the tank **freezes**: its share of the raw water (tank size over raw room) is locked in ice, out of reach and not counted as spilled, and its capacity leaves the raw pool, so it takes no new water. On thaw the ice returns to stock; anything beyond room spills normally. The ledger records the net change as `frozen`, so start + produced + purchased + supplied − consumed − spilled − spoiled − processed − sold − frozen = end every day. Freeze and thaw events reach the Almanac (Weather) and toasts.

The tank card offers two protections, paid up front, installed with the household's spare labor, and never refunded:

- **Insulate** ($40 + $0.25/gal, 2 h): multiplies the tolerance by 4.
- **Add heater** ($60, 1 h): on freezing days draws 0.0008 kWh per gallon per °F below 32 °F. A fully powered heater reverses that day's frost; partial power slows it; no power and it freezes like a bare tank. The heater uses electricity left after household use (stock, battery, today's output); the grid backstop does not run it.

All quantities are labeled experimental game balance in `data/catalog_overrides.json` (`winterStorage`), validated by the exporter. They are not engineering forecasts for a given tank.

## Controlled results

On the matched remote-job first-year route, LL-33 changes no survival outcome at any site or weather: zero survival-short days and the same 75.2 minimum wellbeing in all 24 runs. Raw water spill falls from 193–986 gal a year to at most 1.3 gal, because drinking water no longer occupies the shared tank. Drinking water spill rises to 208.6 gal: the starter filter's 2 gal/day exceeds two adults' ~1.4 gal/day once the 50 gal of containers are full. The barrel freezes for 148–153 tank-days in Laramie and 36–66 on the Front Range, locking about 54 gal at a time; Asheville never freezes. End cash is $14–$168 lower. In Laramie Standard ($129 lower) the cause is 31 extra hours of water fetching (refilling raw room the drinking water used to occupy, and refilling after freezes), which displaces about 6 paid hours; market spending is unchanged.

The Laramie winter checks show insulation is not enough there (a barrel still freezes by day 3; an IBC by day 7), while a powered heater keeps both liquid for 27.7 and 138.6 kWh over January–April. On the Front Range insulation alone prevents freezing. With 10 cu ft of bins only 95 lb of a 500 lb hay order is delivered (and the feed in the same order finds no room); a shed stores the whole order.

## Verification

Thirteen engine regressions cover pool separation against the legacy spill, container overflow, the 7-day water quest, feed limits with and without a shed, the market clamp, freezing and thawing in Laramie with conservation, frozen capacity, insulation labor and cost, heater power and its absence, refusals, legacy digests with the gate off, releasing ice when the gate is turned off, and replay/JSON continuation. Three catalog tests reject invalid tank and feed rules. One browser test enables the setting, freezes a barrel through a Front Range winter, insulates it through the card, and checks the drinking water page.

## Limits

Daily temperatures follow the site's monthly degree-day shape, so there are no cold snaps within a month beyond Real weather's yearly swing. Tanks don't burst, pipes and pumps don't freeze, and snow doesn't change rain capture. Drinking water containers can't be expanded until LL-40. Feed spoilage, rodents and hay quality aren't modeled. Removing a frozen tank discards its ice.
