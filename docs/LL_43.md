# LL-43: livestock lifecycle infrastructure

Implemented October 25, 2026 in engine **0.22.0**. Four workbook-backed systems added with `scripts/catalog_patch.py`; existing typed workbook values remain unchanged. See the [workbook diff](catalog_patches/ll43-livestock-diff.md) and [controlled comparisons](LL_43_COMPARISONS.md). No default settings change.

| System | Catalog row | Integration |
|---|---|---|
| Egg Incubator (24 eggs) | 4 sq ft, 0.1 h/week upkeep | Sets 24 eggs from stock; each of 21 counted days needs 0.5 kWh and 80% upkeep; ~70% hatch into an LL-31 Baby Chicks cohort |
| Broody Hen Nest Box | 4 sq ft, 0.05 h/week, 10 lb lumber once | Sets 8 eggs under the nearest free mature hen within 30 ft; ~85% hatch; she stops laying while she sits |
| Predator-Proof Run (12 birds) | 120 sq ft, 0.25 h/week, 150 lb lumber once | While staffed, protects whole poultry cohorts within 20 ft from LL-31 predator losses, up to 12 birds per run |
| Rotational Paddock Kit | no footprint, 1 h/week moving | Placed on a pasture, raises its Hay and Green biomass by up to 15% in proportion to yesterday's moving labor |

Hatching and the run need **Settings → Experimental mechanics → Livestock lifecycle**, because chicks, maturity and predators exist only there. The paddock kit works whenever placed on a pasture.

## Hatching

A clutch is a player order (**Set eggs** on the card, logged as a `hatch` action). It needs a mature laying hen cohort on the parcel, which stands in for a fertile flock. A nest box also needs a free mature hen within 30 ft and at least 13 hours of daylight, so hens go broody in spring and summer only.

The clutch waits until the full number of eggs is in stock. While it waits, those eggs are held back from meals, like seed held for planting. They are then drawn once as `processed`, not eaten. Each day after that counts toward 21 only if:

- **Incubator:** 80% of its upkeep was met (times LL-28 condition) and 0.5 kWh was available after the household's own use. That power is drawn as `consumed`.
- **Nest box:** her flock is still active.

More than two missed days loses the eggs (`hatch-failed`). Culling, losing or retiring the sitting hen also ends the clutch. On day 21, floor(eggs × hatch rate) chicks are placed as a Baby Chicks cohort, as LL-31 places a birth. If the parcel has no room, the clutch waits without drawing more power. The chicks grow into hens through the existing LL-31 maturity rules.

A sitting hen's cohort lays (scale − sitting hens) / scale of its normal eggs. With six one-hen cohorts, one sitting hen costs about 10–11 eggs over a June clutch at all three sites.

## Predator-proof run

A run joins the guardian and secure housing as LL-31 protection. Coverage is assigned run by run, in placement order: a run takes each poultry cohort within 20 ft (center to center) whose birds still fit in its 12-bird room. A cohort larger than the room it has left is not covered at all. A run counts only when 80% of its upkeep was met on the previous day. The card shows the birds its working runs cover. Warnings still come; the run prevents the loss when the predator returns.

## Rotational paddocks

The kit has no footprint and is placed on a pasture, like the weatherization retrofit on a house. The pasture's Hay and Green biomass output is multiplied by 1 + 0.15 × yesterday's moving labor met × LL-28 condition. Several kits on one pasture count once. A kit off a pasture, or with no labor, gives nothing. Its first day gives nothing, because no moving has been measured yet.

## Quantities and evidence

Chicken eggs hatch in about 21 days ([TNAU Agritech](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/animal_husbandry/ani_chik_hatching%20of%20eggs.html)). A standard hen covers about 6–12 eggs ([Hobby Farms](https://www.hobbyfarms.com/how-to-work-with-a-broody-hen/)); the game sets 8. Hatch rates (70% incubator, 85% hen), 0.5 kWh/day, the two-missed-day tolerance, 13 h of daylight, the run's 12 birds and 20 ft, and all costs are experimental game assumptions.

The evidence for rotational grazing is mixed. [UF/IFAS](https://rcrec-ona.ifas.ufl.edu/news-and-publications/in-focus-archives/forage/grazing-methods-impact-on-forage-cattle-production/) reports more forage under rotation on bahiagrass. A [rangeland review](https://bioone.org/journals/rangeland-ecology-and-management/volume-61/issue-1/06-159R.1/Rotational-Grazing-on-Rangelands--Reconciliation-of-Perception-and-Experimental/10.2111/06-159R.1.full) found production equal or greater under continuous grazing in most experiments. The 15% bonus is a conservative game assumption, not a forecast.

## Measured

`docs/LL_43_COMPARISONS.md`, reproduced with `node --import tsx packages/cli/bots/husbandry.ts`:

- **One June clutch** (six hens, all three sites): the incubator turns 24 eggs and 10.5 kWh into 16 chicks; the nest box turns 8 eggs into 6 chicks for about 10–11 eggs not laid; the existing $5 breeding service gives one chick per hen booked.
- **A year of predators** (seeds 1–3, all sites): without protection, 5–6 hens are lost; a staffed run nearby loses none and prevents 16–21 attacks for 13.04 h of upkeep.
- **A rotated 1-acre pasture**: 1,564 → 1,798 lb hay and 1,043 → 1,199 lb green biomass for 52 h of moving labor. The existing pasture row's output does not vary with site or weather, so all three sites match.

Fixtures supply household food, water and cash and disable wellbeing drift. No survival-route gain is claimed.

## Tests

Catalog: rule validation and the four workbook rows. Browser: setting eggs on an incubator card; a paddock card showing its measured factor.

## Limits

No rooster, egg fertility or egg age; a mature hen stands in for a fertile flock. Every chick in a clutch hatches on day 21. No humidity, turning or candling. A nest box picks the nearest free hen at setting time. The run has no separate roof/door state, and the paddock models no forage rest periods, stocking rate or soil response. The existing pasture still ignores weather. LL-44 is next.
