Everything uses the existing **Settings → Experimental mechanics → Selling and trade** gate except spinning, which is an ordinary workbook system and works whenever placed.

## CSA

The CSA's produce, packing labor and income are growing-season flows (a timing override; annual totals keep workbook parity), so it asks for nothing in winter. Members collect on the farm, so it has no transport input. Each growing-season day, the CSA earns its Capital output for the share of boxes packed (produce and labor met). The missing share is refunded by that lower income, and half of the missing revenue is paid on top as a make-good. Under the cash floor or finance, the make-good never exceeds the cash on hand. Make-goods are a "CSA make-good" bill, part of total spending.

Boxes are allocated before household meals each day, but nothing reserves stored produce for later boxes. Because the household eats its most perishable food first, stored produce is shared with meals. A CSA needs a steady harvest, not a stockpile.

## Spinning and weaving

Wool previously had only LL-29's raw sale outlet ($4/lb reference). Spinning turns it into **Woolen goods** (canonical lbs, storable, sale-only reference $25/lb, not buyable), with 10% lost in washing, carding and spinning, and adds wellbeing. Woolen goods use the existing sale and barter queue.

## Labor barter

After the tutorial, the market's barter form offers "Labor (hours of spare work for Ruth)". The order is the existing `sell` action with `resource: 'Labor'` and a goods choice; cash is not allowed. Each day it fills from spare household labor only (after upkeep, gathering and construction, at least half an hour free) and the rest waits. Each hour buys $12 of goods at their reference prices. Ruth's 70% trade terms are already included in that rate. The hours appear as "Neighbor work".

## Measured

`docs/LL_46_COMPARISONS.md`, reproduced with `node --import tsx packages/cli/bots/income.ts`, Standard seed 1:

- **A CSA season:** fully supplied, it earns $2,346 for 104 h of packing at every site; the annual total is the same everywhere and spreads over 98 (Laramie) to 184 (Asheville) growing days. With no produce, it pays $1,173 in make-goods. A half-season stockpile lasts about three weeks, because the household eats it too: net −$599 to −$811.
- **A year of wool** (26 lb): 23.4 lb of woolen goods for 208 h; sold at the 50% rate, $292.50 against $52 for the raw wool, or $1.16 of added value an hour. Spinning is a wellbeing and barter outlet more than an income.
- **Spare work for Ruth:** 48 h over 12 summer weeks bought 1,646 lb of chicken feed ($576 of goods) with no cash.

Fixtures supply household food, water and cash and disable wellbeing drift. No survival-route gain is claimed.

## Quantities and evidence

The $45 share price, make-good factor, spinning rate and loss, the $25/lb reference and the $12/hour barter rate are experimental game balance, labeled in the workbook and override notes. No outside quantity is cited.

## Tests

Unit: a fully packed CSA week earns its promise with no make-good; a week with no produce earns nothing and pays half as a make-good, matching the bill and the card; the cash floor caps the make-good; no CSA activity in winter or with the gate off; spinning's loss and wellbeing; woolen goods selling at their reference; no spinning without wool; labor barter locked until the tutorial ends and goods-only; partial fills from spare hours; replay and JSON continuation. Catalog: workbook rows, growing-season timings, the new resource, the spinning wheel as the only wool consumer, rule validation. Browser: a CSA card showing a make-good, and queuing labor for chicken feed with Ruth.

## Limits

CSA members never cancel or renew, there is no box mix and no produce reserve. Spinning abstracts washing, carding and dyeing into one rate. Ruth has unlimited goods and work. A spatial-cache bypass was added whenever a CSA is placed, because the cached layout is not keyed on the trade setting. LL-47 (catalog audit gates) is next.
