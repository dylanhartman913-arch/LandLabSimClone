# LL-36: household composition and skills

Implemented October 24, 2026 in engine **0.16.0**. This completes one ticket, LL-36. Enable **Settings → Experimental mechanics → Household roles, skills and health** to try it. New games and continuation migrations leave the setting off. [Measured comparisons](LL_36_COMPARISONS.md) reproduce 24 first-year runs and a household composition table.

## Roles

Before LL-36 everyone was an identical adult. Under the gate each person's card has a **role**:

| Role | Needs (share of an adult) | Labor (share of an adult) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Adult | 100% | 100% |
| Child | 70% | 25% |
| Elder | 90% | 50% |

Needs scale every input of the catalog Human Being (food, water, supplies, shelter request) and the outputs that follow them (waste, greywater); labor scales the person's contribution to the household pool. Children take no paid work: the LL-23 employment cap counts adults at 40 h a week and elders at their labor share. Adults are pinned to the catalog Human Being by the exporter.

## Skills

Each person builds three skills with practice. Every day's hours of building (resident construction), growing (upkeep of gardens) and gathering are shared among the people working, in proportion to their labor that day. A skill's bonus is max × (1 − 2^(−hours ÷ half-learned hours)):

| Skill | Effect | Max | Half-learned after |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Building | DIY progress per hour (outside crews unchanged) | +50% | 150 h |
| Growing | Garden harvests | +25% | 100 h |
| Gathering | Yield per hour at the land's nodes | +30% | 100 h |

The household uses the labor-weighted average of everyone working, so a skilled person who is ill, injured or in town stops contributing their skill. Gathering skill still draws on the same finite nodes (LL-34), and growing skill multiplies whatever fertility allows (LL-35).

## Illness and injury

- **Illness:** a person below 40 wellbeing may fall ill, with a daily chance rising linearly to 2% at wellbeing 0. An ill person does no work for 7 days.
- **Injury:** dangerous work carries a risk per hour: 0.05% per hour of resident building and 0.2% per hour of chainsaw work (LL-34). On an injury, one worker (chosen by labor weight) is on light duty, at 30% labor, for 14 days.
- **Draws:** these use their own saved random stream (`householdRng`), so they never change the weather or the predator draws.
- **Visibility:** illness, injury and recovery reach the Almanac (People) and toasts. The person card shows today's illness chance when wellbeing is low (the precursor warning) and the days left to recover.

All quantities are experimental game balance in `data/catalog_overrides.json` (`householdRoles`), validated by the exporter. The workbook is unchanged.

## Controlled results

On the matched remote-job route for the default two-adult household, no run gains a survival-short day and wellbeing never drops below 40, so nobody falls ill. With about 89 resident building hours, the injury risk stays small and no injury occurs. Practice is dominated by water fetching: the best gathering bonus reaches 28–29% by year end, building 9% and growing 4%. Faster fetching frees hours for the paid job, so end cash is $1,396–$2,030 higher on the Front Range, $1,107–$1,671 in Laramie and $83–$123 in Asheville.

The composition table holds needs fixed (validation supply) and changes only the third person. Food requested is 42,000 kcal a week for three adults, 37,800 with a child and 40,600 with an elder. Household labor is 141, 106 and 118 h a week, and the paid-work cap is 120, 80 and 100 h. A child costs 70% of an adult's upkeep and gives a quarter of the work, so a household with children needs more self-reliance per worker.

I tried adding a third person to the two-adult remote-job route, but that route collapses with any third person, adult included, under legacy rules too (37% shelter, 33% sanitation), so it isn't a fair test of roles; it is not in the comparison.

## Verification

Nine engine regressions cover role needs and labor, the person card's role-scaled inputs, the employment cap with a child and an elder, faster DIY with building skill, practice attribution and the gathering bonus, the growing bonus on a garden, illness stopping and restoring work, illness only below the wellbeing threshold, illness and injury risk with the weather stream unchanged, the person card view, and unchanged legacy digests plus replay/JSON continuation. A shared-update shortcut in the wellbeing step would have copied one person's role, skills or illness onto a housemate; it now compares those fields. Two catalog tests pin adults to the catalog and reject unknown systems. One browser test makes a person a child through the card and checks practice building up.

## Limits

No aging between roles, births or new household members beyond placing a person; no individual task assignment (skills are shared out by labor); no medical care or first aid to shorten recovery (LL-38 mitigations); and the start screen still creates adults only (LL-49 households).
