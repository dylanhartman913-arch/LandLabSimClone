# LL-42: animal feed and fodder

Implemented October 25, 2026 in engine **0.21.0**. Three workbook-backed systems added with `scripts/catalog_patch.py`; existing typed workbook values remain unchanged. See the [workbook diff](catalog_patches/ll42-feed-fodder-diff.md) and [controlled comparisons](LL_42_COMPARISONS.md). No dependencies or default settings change.

| System | Catalog inputs and output | Integration |
|---|---|---|
| Hay Meadow (1/10 acre) | 4,356 sq ft ground footprint, Rainfall, 0.3 h/week Labor → 4 lb/week Hay nominal | Growing-season harvest, rainfall/drought scaling; LL-34 scythe saves cutting labor |
| Poultry Feed Mix | 7 lb Grain + 2 lb Green biomass + 1 lb Grubs + 1 h Labor per week → 9 lb Chicken feed | Actual input-limited production, 1 lb processing loss; feeds existing livestock from stock |
| Hay Barn | 100 sq ft footprint → 200 cu ft dedicated Feed storage, 0.1 h/week upkeep | Joins LL-33's shared hay/feed pool without adding tool space |

The meadow and mix work when placed, without a new experiment toggle. Scythe assistance uses **Settings → Experimental mechanics → Tools multiply gathering**. Barn capacity uses **Separate storage and winter water**. The barn's usable capacity is passive physical storage; missing labor accelerates wear under LL-28 rather than instantly emptying it. Removing the barn or reducing its condition removes its corresponding room, including from market delivery checks.

## Meadow and tools

The meadow occupies a real 66 × 66 ft ground parcel. It cannot overlap another ground planting or fit on a parcel smaller than its footprint. Objects can sit on that ground under the existing placement rules. No extra Land-area capacity output is invented, and land is not charged twice through a second capacity input.

Hay is spread across the site's growing season rather than produced through winter. At the reference 15 inches annual precipitation the annual ceiling is about 209 lb; drier sites multiply it by annual precipitation / 15, capped at 1. Real-year drought adds the existing precipitation multiplier, bounded between 20% and 100%. Existing growing-season weather supplies frost/hail effects. No rain ambient, zero annual rainfall or no household labor stops production. Annual spreadsheet potential remains the nominal ceiling; actual cards, resource pages and ledgers show realized production.

The workbook's 0.3 h/week Labor includes 0.1 h upkeep plus 0.2 h hand cutting. Upkeep continues through winter; cutting follows the seasonal hay share and rainfall. An enabled, active scythe applies its existing LL-34 2.4× rate to cutting only. The meadow does not make more hay merely because a scythe is owned. A missing, disabled or failed scythe supplies no benefit. LL-28 condition reduces the extra speed.

Scythes share the existing eight-hour daily tool budget across all meadows and node gathering. Meadow cutting reserves time first, proportionally across meadow area/scale; any remainder can gather greens. The reservation is conservative: if actual meadow work is curtailed later, the unused reservation is not reassigned to a gathering pass that already happened. A second gathering pass from reused idle labor cannot reset that budget. Actual cutting, tool hours and saved hand hours are recorded in optional daily `fodder` entries and shown on the meadow card.

## Feed and storage

Feed mixing requires all three ingredients in the specified ratio. Missing input or labor stops the batch and leaves the other ingredients unprocessed; partial supply scales every draw and the output together. Production lands after ordinary consumption, so birds can use newly made feed the next day. Ingredient draws use the existing `processed` ledger field, separately from household food consumption. Grain used here does not add a household Food need or inflate grown calories. The ten-to-nine mass ratio leaves an explicit processing loss rather than creating feed from nothing.

The mix closes Chicken feed's market-only source gap using resources already produced by the grain plot, biomass sources and insect systems. It does not turn grain into a new purchased feed supply or change those existing producers. Feed still has to reach the birds along with their water and other operating inputs.

The barn's new **Feed storage** resource has one canonical unit, **cu ft**. It joins free general Storage space plus household bins under LL-33. Existing densities remain 9.5 lb hay/cu ft and 40 lb chicken feed/cu ft. With one barn and empty household bins, 1,900 lb hay plus 400 lb feed exactly fill 210 cu ft. These are shared limits; each resource does not get its own full barn. The barn supplies no general Storage space, so tools cannot occupy it. Output beyond room spills, orders wait for room, and resource pages display the same pool.

## Quantities and evidence

[CSU Extension's rural living guide](https://boulder.extension.colostate.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2021/03/BoCo-Rural-Living-Guide-Agriculture-Rev-1.pdf) gives a dryland grass hay range of 0.5–1.5 tons/acre. About 200 lb on 0.1 acre lies within that range. The chosen rainfall response, seasonal smoothing, hand-cut hours and all costs remain explicit experimental game assumptions. [Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/how-much-does-that-bale-really-weigh-feeding-and-fertilizer-implications) explains variable bale density; this ticket preserves LL-33's existing densities and labels usable barn volume as a game assumption.

[University of Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/animals-and-livestock/poultry/raising-chickens-for-eggs) describes the need for balanced poultry feed. The game's grain/greens/grubs ratio and 10% processing loss abstract nutrient balancing; they are not a livestock ration recommendation. No nutritional formula is claimed from that source.

Across 24 paired full-year fixtures (three sites, Standard seed 1 and Real seeds 1–3, hand/scythe), scythes leave fully staffed hay yield unchanged. Front Range Standard produces 208.57 lb and saves 6.08 cutting hours; meadow labor falls 15.64 → 9.56 h. The scythe itself costs 5.21 h/year upkeep, so buying one only for this small meadow has little net labor benefit and can cost extra labor in a poor year. Laramie Standard produces 152.95 lb because the site is drier. These results do not tune the catalog or claim survival-route improvements.

The full feed batch produces 9 lb in seven days from 7 lb grain, 2 lb greens and 1 lb grubs with 1 h labor; missing any ingredient yields zero. Barn delivery admits 1,900 lb hay and 400 lb feed together; household bins alone admit 95 lb hay and leave the rest queued. Comparisons use supplied household stocks and disabled wellbeing drift to isolate equipment.

Regression coverage includes parcel footprint, winter upkeep, rain/drought/labor failure, absent/disabled/broken scythes, shared tool limits including a second idle-labor pass, proportional lossy mixing, separate meal accounting, next-day feeding, overflow, shared densities, market room, failed/removed barn capacity, immutable input state and deterministic JSON continuation. Browser flows verify measured cutting savings and the mixed feed/barn resource page. Existing catalog/parity/pacing and legacy digest assertions remain intact; catalog count/modding fixtures advance to the new row counts and next unused IDs.

Limits: no individual mowing dates, rain-free drying window, moisture accounting, nutritional/mineral inventory, hay spoilage or barn fire. Hay is smoothed over the growing season. Scythe reservations can leave unused tool time after curtailment. The existing pasture remains unchanged. LL-43 and later Phase 10 source-audit work are still open.
