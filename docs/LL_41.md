# LL-41: food processing and preservation

Implemented October 25, 2026, engine **0.20.0**. Enable **Settings → Experimental mechanics → Food preservation chain**. Reuses LL-30's Pressure Canner, Solar Dehydrator, standing preservation orders and actual-flow accounting. Adds six workbook systems through `catalog_patch.py`, a Cheese resource in pounds (1,800 kcal/lb) and rodent-protected Grain storage in pounds. No dependencies or default setting changes. [Controlled comparisons](LL_41_COMPARISONS.md); workbook audit: `catalog_patches/ll41-food-processing-diff.md`.

| System | Inputs and output |
|---|---|
| Water-Bath Canner | Acidified produce, Cooking fuel and spare Labor → Preserved food; 6,000 input kcal/day ceiling, 90% yield |
| Pressure Canner (existing) | Vegetables, roots, meat or fish; unchanged 12,000 kcal/day ceiling, 90% yield |
| Solar Dehydrator (existing) | Produce, roots, meat, fish or mushrooms; unchanged 6,000 kcal/day ceiling, sunlight limits work |
| Electric Dehydrator | Same input family as solar drying; 6,000 input kcal/day, 90% yield, 4.8 kWh for a full batch |
| Smokehouse | Meat or fish and Woody biomass; 6,000 input kcal/day, 85% yield, 3 lb wood for a full batch |
| Cheese Press | 1.25 gal Milk and 1 h Labor per week → 1 lb Cheese; slower spoilage than milk |
| Grain Bin | 10 cu ft Food storage plus 450 lb rodent-protected Grain storage; 0.1 h/week upkeep |
| Seed Saving Station | 1 lb seed-bearing garden produce and 0.5 h Labor per week → 1 oz Seeds |

All recipe processing consumes food and energy already on hand or produced that day, after ordinary allocation. Spare labor, remaining energy, actual processor upkeep, condition, scaling, input reserve and planting reserve limit batches. Standing rules remain editable on the card. None run without a selected input and enabled food-preservation gate. Missing energy/food/labor pauses work. Smokehouse wood joins the recipe energy-resource enum; it is not transformed into free cooking energy.

The cheese press and seed station use their workbook input/output rates automatically when active and the preservation gate is enabled; pause by removing the station or disabling the gate. Inputs are recorded as **processed**, separately from meals. Their food inputs do not add to household Food demand. Cheese production is labeled **converted**, so it does not inflate grown calories or self-reliance. The calorie difference between milk and cheese leaves the food pool as unrecovered whey. Cheese can feed residents through the existing food substitution mechanism and spoils at 1%/week before cold-storage reduction, compared with milk's 30%/week. No ripening timer is modeled.

With food preservation enabled, grain outside operating Grain storage suffers an added 1% weekly rodent loss, in addition to existing spoilage. Protection is limited to actual staffed capacity; a bin without upkeep labor does not protect grain. Rodent losses use the existing spoilage ledger. Grain remains in the shared Food storage pool; bin capacity also contributes 10 cu ft there. Overflow and rodents remain separate losses. Cold storage alone slows ordinary spoilage but does not prevent rodents.

Seed saving draws the existing combined garden produce resource rather than creating Seeds from nothing. The one-pound-to-one-ounce conversion is a mixed-crop game assumption; cultivar compatibility, flowering, isolation and biennial seed cycles are abstracted. This creates a home source for the Seeds consumed by existing gardens; seed potatoes and Seedlings remain distinct inputs.

## Sources and measurements

[NMSU Extension](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_e/E216/index.html) supplies the 1.25-gallon-per-pound cheese ratio. [Excalibur's model comparison](https://excaliburdehydratorsaustralia.com/pages/model-comparison) lists a 600 W dehydrator; the assumed eight-hour batch gives 4.8 kWh. Other throughput, yield, labor, price and shelf-life values are explicitly experimental balance; row notes distinguish them from these references.

Water-bath canning accepts only the combined produce resource, interpreted as its **acidified fruit/vegetable portion**. It rejects meat and roots. Acidity and additives are not tracked. [National Center for Home Food Preservation](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/general-canning-information/ensuring-safe-canned-foods/) distinguishes acid-food boiling-water canning from low-acid pressure canning. Game batch values are not food-preservation instructions. The smokehouse abstraction includes curing and drying, rather than asserting that smoking alone creates shelf-stable food.

Full controlled batches produce 5,400 preserved kcal from 6,000 fresh kcal for water-bath/electric drying, and 5,100 for smoking; electric drying spends 4.8 kWh and smoking 3 lb wood. Over 30 days the grain-bin fixture loses 0.136 lb to spoilage/rodents versus 3.454 lb with a root cellar alone; both households eat 40 lb grain, and storage does not bind. Seven days of processing uses 1.25 gal milk for 1 lb cheese and 1 lb garden output for 1 oz seed. These are isolated equipment checks, not claims of first-year household survival.

Regression tests cover input/energy shortages, gate behavior, losses and conservation, water-bath restrictions, conversion versus harvest/meal accounting, cheese spoilage, staffed grain protection and seed conversion/continuation. Existing LL-30 recipe tests remain unchanged. The browser test starts an electric drying rule through the UI and checks a measured batch. Legacy numeric digests remain unchanged; unused expansion resources are omitted only from the existing compact legacy balance serializer.

Limits: no jars/lids, acid inventory, curing salt, whey recovery, ripening calendar, cultivar selection or individual seed-bank capacity. The combined produce resource remains a coarse abstraction. The catalog audit still lists sources that later Phase 10 tickets address; LL-40/41 do not claim to finish the phase-wide two-source goal.
