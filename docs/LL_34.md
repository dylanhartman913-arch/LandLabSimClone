# LL-34: tools multiply gathering

Implemented October 24, 2026 in engine **0.14.0**. This completes one ticket, LL-34. Enable **Settings → Experimental mechanics → Tools multiply gathering** to try it. New games and continuation migrations leave the setting off. [Measured comparisons](LL_34_COMPARISONS.md) reproduce 24 first-year runs and 6 winter firewood checks.

## The rule

Before LL-34 the Chainsaw, Scythe and Broadfork were standalone producers: a chainsaw made 150 lb of wood a week from 2.2 labor hours, whether or not the land had any deadfall. Under the gate they make nothing on their own. Instead each multiplies the yield per hour of one gathering job at the land's own nodes:

| Tool | Job | Multiplies | × | Fuel | Derivation |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Chainsaw | Gather firewood | Woody biomass at deadfall | 1.7 | 0.091 gal gasoline per working hour | Catalog row: 150 lb per 2.2 h ≈ 68 lb/h vs 40 lb/h by hand; 0.2 gal per 2.2 h |
| Scythe | Forage | Green biomass at wild greens | 2.4 | none | Catalog row: 30 lb per 2.1 h ≈ 14 lb/h vs 6 lb/h by hand |
| Broadfork | Dig clay and soil | Soil at the clay bank | 2 | none | Capped: the catalog row implies 13×, but a broadfork loosens beds rather than digging clay |

Walking to and from the node is not sped up, so the saving in total hours is smaller than the multiplier: about 41% fewer firewood hours per pound with a chainsaw. Harvest draws down the same finite node stock as hand gathering, so a tool can never yield more than the land holds; it only gets there faster. By-products are not multiplied (a scythe does not forage more greens and berries for the table).

Each tool covers up to 8 working hours a day (one person, one tool). Fuel comes from stock: with too little, the boost applies only to the share of hours the fuel covers, and with none there is no boost. Under LL-28 wear, the extra yield is scaled by the tool's condition factor, so a worn-out tool gathers at the hand rate. A tool's recurring labor falls to its catalog upkeep hours (0.2 h a week for the chainsaw), and its fuel input moves from a weekly flow to the per-hour draw above. The card's inputs and outputs show these gated values.

Gathering only happens when a job has a target. The firewood, water and food targets on the Work panel work as before. If the household owns a tool and no target covers its resource, the job keeps about a week of what the household actually uses (for example a compost pile's greens or a stove's wood), topped up at most two days' use per day.

All tool quantities are experimental game balance in `data/catalog_overrides.json` (`toolRules`), validated by the exporter against the workbook rows (the tool must make the resource and use the fuel). The workbook is unchanged.

## Controlled results

On the matched remote-job first-year route (a chainsaw and a gasoline standing order bought on day 1 in both variants), no run gains a survival-short day. The legacy chainsaw adds 7,020 lb of wood a year from nothing, while the real deadfall ends the year all but exhausted (2–18 lb left) in both variants. Under the gate that phantom wood is gone, the same deadfall supplies 1,181–12,320 lb, and firewood hours fall by 39–41% at every site (Front Range 134 → 81 h). The freed hours go to the paid job (37 more paid hours on the Front Range Standard run, 13 in Laramie), so end cash is $880–$1,126 higher on the Front Range and between −$95 and +$269 elsewhere. Gasoline use follows the work: 1.6 gal in Laramie, 6.9 on the Front Range and 16.5 in Asheville, against a flat 10.4 gal before.

In the winter checks (three stoves, January–April), a chainsaw cuts the hours per 100 lb from about 2.6 to 1.5 for the same wood. In Laramie both variants run out: the site's single 600 lb deadfall regrows 1.6 lb a day, and stoves are short of wood for 90 of 120 days with or without the saw. Laramie firewood has to be bought or grown; a tool cannot fix that.

## Verification

Ten engine regressions cover the legacy standalone output, the gated card and spatial factors, the 1.7× rate at the same deadfall with fuel accounting, no fuel and running dry, the finite-node ceiling, a worn-out tool, the scythe's greens target and its non-effect on foraged food, a chainsaw without a firewood target, the tool card's ledger totals, unchanged legacy digests and replay/JSON continuation. Three catalog tests reject a tool rule for a resource the system doesn't make, fuel it doesn't use, duplicates and multipliers below 1. One browser test enables the setting, adds a stove and a chainsaw, buys gasoline through the market and checks the card against the ledger.

## Limits

One multiplier per tool, no skill effect (LL-36), no tool sharing across jobs, and no separate wear from hours of use beyond LL-28's model. The Wood Chipper is a processor, not a gathering tool, and is unchanged. The broadfork's catalog role in garden beds is not modeled here (LL-35 soil fertility). Gathering for one-time construction materials is still covered by imports, not by tools.
