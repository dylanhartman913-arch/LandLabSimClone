# LL-40: water infrastructure

Implemented October 25, 2026, engine **0.20.0**. Five workbook systems, added through `scripts/catalog_patch.py`, with canonical units and regenerated catalog/goldens. Existing workbook numbers remain unchanged; see `catalog_patches/ll40-water-diff.md`. No new dependencies or default settings. [Controlled comparisons](LL_40_COMPARISONS.md) isolate equipment across the three sites.

| System | Behavior | Required setting |
|---|---|---|
| Spring Box with Gravity Line | 175 gal/week nominal raw Water, 0.25 h/week upkeep. Connects to the nearest in-season spring within 50 ft. Actual flow is limited by site stock and drought. All boxes share a spring's daily flow budget. | None |
| First-Flush Diverter | After a fully staffed day, discards 5% of captured roof runoff and reduces filter cleaning labor by 25% when rain capture is installed. Does not replace filtering. | Separate storage and winter water |
| Potable Water Tank (100 gal) | Adds 100 gal to the safe-water pool only. No raw capacity and no water production. Assumed indoors/frost-protected. | Separate storage and winter water |
| Heat-Traced Water Tank (100 gal) | Outdoor raw tank with built-in heating, installed at purchase. Uses the LL-33 freezing model and 0.0008 kWh/gal/freezing-degree-day. Without spare power it can freeze. | Separate storage and winter water |
| Water Test Kit | Measured labor and reagent spending enable a precautionary contamination warning about untreated sources. Warning appears on the kit card and checklist. | Warned risk events |

Spring boxes output untreated Water and draw from the same finite stock as gathering. A site's infinite spring still has a shared daily flow ceiling: one hour-equivalent of its site yield, nominally 25 gal/day. The limit follows drought-sensitive site yields, including their existing 20% floor. Moving outside the radius, removing the node, an out-of-season node, or missing upkeep stops output. The connection is shown on the card. There is no elevation/topographic model: “gravity” is a placement abstraction, not a surveyed head calculation.

The diverter's benefit uses previous-day measured satisfaction, like an installed mitigation becoming ready after a working day. Multiple diverters do not stack. Because raw water is pooled, its filter-labor benefit applies to household water filters when rain capture is installed; it does not trace rainwater molecules separately. Its own upkeep can exceed the small filter-labor saving. Winter freezing can stop the rain barrel entirely; the comparisons show that lower filter labor does not imply more potable production.

The heat-traced tank has fixed built-in protection; the retrofit buttons are hidden and attempts to remove its trace through an action are rejected. Heater consumption, freezing, thawing and conservation use the existing LL-33 records. Extra potable capacity is respected by daily storage, water boiling, resource views and market delivery room checks.

The kit does not invent a pathogen concentration, pronounce a sample safe, purify water or start an illness lottery. It warns that an untreated source may contain contaminants and names filtering or boiling as the action. No labor or reagent budget means no warning record; switching off risk events hides it. Warnings are ordinary saved daily ledger records and survive JSON continuation. No new action or migration is required.

## Evidence and references

The 100-gal potable tank keeps exactly 100 additional gallons in every site fixture. A connected box delivers 175 raw gallons over seven days for 0.25 h upkeep; Asheville has no spring and receives zero. The heat trace eliminates 25 frozen tank-days on the Front Range and 98 in Laramie in the 120-day checks, using 3.0 and 50.4 kWh respectively. These controlled camps have food/cash supplied and wellbeing drift disabled; they are not first-year survival-route claims.

Spring collection, sealing, contamination and testing rationale: [Penn State Extension, Spring Development and Protection](https://extension.psu.edu/spring-development-and-protection). First-flush design rationale: [Rain Harvesting installation guide](https://www.rainharvest.com/info/rainharvesting/RainHarvesting-Delta-First-Flush-Post-Wall-Installation-Guide.pdf). These references support system behavior, not the chosen game prices/rates. Costs, labor, volumes, distance and percentages are explicitly labeled experimental balance in workbook row notes and validated overrides.

Tests cover absent/out-of-range/out-of-season springs, shared flow, finite stock, drought, missing labor, separate safe-water capacity, powered/unpowered freezing, energy/water conservation, first-flush readiness, filter-card quantities, paid/unfunded kit operation, gate opt-out and JSON continuation. The browser test places a kit through the store, enables the setting through the UI and verifies both warning surfaces.

Limits: no pipes freezing, pressure/head calculation, per-tank contaminant chemistry, lab certification or contamination-induced illness. Safe-water tanks are assumed indoors. First-flush readiness is a one-day measurement and source mixing remains pooled.
