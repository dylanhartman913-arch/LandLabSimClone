# LL-35: soil fertility

Implemented October 24, 2026 in engine **0.15.0**. This completes one ticket, LL-35. Enable **Settings → Experimental mechanics → Soil fertility** to try it. New games and continuation migrations leave the setting off. [Measured comparisons](LL_35_COMPARISONS.md) reproduce 24 first-year runs and six three-year garden checks.

## The rule

Before LL-35, beds took compost "per season" as a required input: with compost they cropped at full yield, without it they stopped, and nothing carried over from one season to the next. Under the gate each garden keeps a **fertility** level, where 1 is the catalog's full yield:

- **Yield follows fertility:** harvest = 30% + 70% × fertility of the catalog yield, capped at 110%. Unfed soil never drops below 30% (native mineralization).
- **Cropping draws it down:** a full year of cropping removes 0.35 from annual beds (raised beds, hoop house, potato patch, grain plot) and 0.15 from perennials (food forest, apple tree, raspberries), only on growing days and in proportion to the realized harvest.
- **Compost puts it back:** the catalog's own compost rate replaces exactly that draw. A fully composted bed at fertility 1 keeps catalog parity. Compost no longer switches a garden off; it feeds fertility.
- **Manure** fills a compost shortfall from stock at half strength (a pound of manure counts as half a pound of compost), for the share of the garden that ran that day.
- **A clover cover crop** that is working (seeded) adds 0.2 a year to gardens within 100 ft, shared by their area (1,000 sq ft of clover fully covers up to 1,000 sq ft of garden).
- **Starting levels:** beds filled with bought soil start at 1, dug-in plots (potato patch, grain plot) at 0.7, new perennial plantings at 0.8. Fertility ranges 0 to 1.25.

Each garden runs as its own group so its fertility is its own. The garden card shows fertility, the yield it gives, the catalog compost that holds it steady, and the last week's compost, manure, gains and draw from the ledger.

All quantities are experimental game balance in `data/catalog_overrides.json` (`soilFertility`), validated by the exporter: every garden rule must have a seasonal or yearly compost input, start at or below the maximum, and the cover crop must exist. The workbook is unchanged.

## Controlled results

On the matched remote-job first-year route, no run gains a survival-short day and no garden is stopped for lack of compost in either variant. Crops grown on the homestead fall by about 18% (Front Range Standard 203 → 167 lb), mostly because the tutorial's dug-in plots start at 0.7 fertility. Gardens end the year at a mean fertility of 0.87–0.88, so the second year would start lower unless the household composts more. End cash moves by −$228 to +$44.

The three-year checks (one raised bed and one potato patch, city water, seed each spring):

- **No care:** legacy rules grow nothing. Under the gate the garden still crops but declines, from 129 to 101 to 79 lb, with bed fertility 0.69 → 0.45 → 0.26.
- **Compost or manure at the catalog rate:** the raised bed holds at 1, the patch slowly rises from 0.7 toward 0.86, and crops rise from 150 to 162 lb (legacy: a flat 182 lb, because legacy has no fertility penalty for the dug-in patch).
- **A clover cover crop alone:** slows the decline (141 → 127 lb) but does not replace compost for this garden area.

## Verification

Eight engine regressions cover catalog parity with compost, cropping without compost and the yield factor, recovery with compost against an unfed bed, manure at half strength with ledger accounting, cover crop range, no draw outside the growing season, the garden card against the ledger, and unchanged legacy digests plus replay/JSON continuation. Two catalog tests reject gardens with no seasonal compost, duplicate rules, starts above the maximum and an unknown cover crop. One browser test enables the setting, places a raised bed, provides water and seed as a player would, and checks fertility falling on the card.

## Limits

One fertility number per garden: no separate nitrogen, phosphorus or organic matter, no pH, and no crop rotation bonus. Compost can't be over-applied above the catalog rate, so only manure and cover crops push fertility above 1. Clover is credited by distance rather than being sown into the bed in the off-season. LL-38's pests and disease are separate.
