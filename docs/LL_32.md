# LL-32: shelter occupancy

Implemented October 24, 2026 in engine **0.12.0**. This completes one ticket, LL-32. Enable **Settings → Experimental mechanics → Shelter occupancy** to try it. New games and continuation migrations leave the setting off. [Measured comparisons](LL_32_COMPARISONS.md) reproduce 36 first-year runs across three sites.

## Assigning and closing

The person card now selects a completed, open shelter. Until explicitly assigned, the household stays together in the first open shelter in placement order. Buying another tent does not automatically move anyone. Each person’s household scale contributes their catalog shelter requirement to their home. Residents share that home’s capacity proportionally; overcrowding lowers only their shelter coverage. A person may explicitly have no shelter. Removing an explicitly assigned home leaves its residents unassigned until the player chooses a replacement.

Shelter cards show assigned residents, living space, present residents’ requirements, yesterday’s actual heat request and receipt, and upkeep. Clicking a resident opens their person card. People in town keep their assignment, but consume no space, heat or cooling while away. Move their assignment before closing their home.

Any shelter with no assigned residents can be closed and reopened. Empty open shelters already have zero heat and cooling demand. Closing also stops other recurring inputs except **10% of catalog labor and capital upkeep**. That share is an explicit experimental game balance policy, not a workbook quantity change. Closed shelters supply no living space and cannot receive assignments. Their physical roof and storage capacities remain. Closing does not sell the asset, refund its cost, cancel its financing bill entirely, or stop calendar wear when wear is separately enabled.

## Local warmth and eligibility

Heat and cooling are routed to occupied, open shelters. Outdoor sources cannot heat a home; fixed sources use the catalog’s edge-to-edge delivery radius; portable sources can serve occupied homes; any source integrated into a shelter serves that shelter alone. Each source’s realized output is divided once, proportionally to reachable shelters’ thermal requests. It cannot be duplicated or borrowed by a distant home. Surplus at one home can spill while another remains cold.

Thermal output retains the engine’s existing one-day supply delay. The saved `shelterThermal` receipt map identifies which home can consume yesterday’s output. Enabling the gate starts with no locally routed receipts, so its first operating day warms up; toggling the gate clears that map. Source output, labor, stock consumption and unmet demand remain in normal resource ledgers. Shelter ledgers record their own actual requests and receipts.

People’s heat and cooling wellbeing terms use their own shelter’s receipts. The warmth quest uses the least-served resident’s heat share, so pooled surplus cannot hide a cold resident, including one with no home. Return-home trials check each resident’s own shelter space and warmth, including all returning people’s demand. This safety rule applies under occupancy even if the older safe-recovery setting is absent. Cold-day project forecasts likewise cap useful heat within each reachable home; spare homes add no demand until assigned.

Balance-mode workbook parity and older replay digests remain unchanged with the gate absent/off. The average-year balance display remains a system-level projection; daily resident wellbeing, quest credit and return eligibility use the per-home simulation.

## Controlled results

All variants use the same greenfield remote-job tutorial policy and buy the same prebuilt spare Bell Tent on day 120. Variants retain legacy rules, enable occupancy, or enable occupancy and close the spare. Other settings match current game defaults; ordinary supplies are used. Each site runs Standard seed 1 and Real seeds 1–3. Later actions can differ because the shared tutorial policy reacts to quest progress.

In Laramie Standard, legacy rules produce **29 survival-short days**, minimum wellbeing **0**, and **28** cold days meeting warmth. Occupancy produces **one survival-short day**, minimum wellbeing **74.4**, and **272** qualifying cold days. Requested first-year heat falls from **62.028 million BTU** to **12.811 million BTU**. The reduction includes other unoccupied shelters built by the tutorial policy, as well as the matched spare. Closing the spare additionally saves about **7.88 upkeep hours** across the year. These are simulator policy comparisons, not household heating predictions or grounds for changing defaults.

The one initial shortage in occupancy runs includes the routed-supply warm-up. The isolated regressions separately prove that enough total heat cannot mask a distant cold resident, shared output is not duplicated, power loss stops subsequent cooling supply, overcrowding is local, condition loss reduces living space, and closed shelter upkeep reaches the actual cash/labor ledger.
